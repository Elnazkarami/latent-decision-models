## Confirmatory analysis (17 subjects)

**Plan (fixed in advance).** Changes from the exploratory plan, each motivated by stage 1:
- **Scoring per run:** each held-out run is scored separately and the scores averaged (`score_mode: per_group`), removing the run-drift bias described under "Reading the scores".
- **Group inference:** a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test of the 17 per-subject scores against chance, with a subject-bootstrap 95% CI.
- **Subject-held-out analysis:** leave-one-subject-out, training on the 18 other subjects (sub-02/04 included as training data only).
- **Primary family:** confidence × {own, observed} × {pre-decision, response-locked, post-decision}, Holm within each analysis. Everything else is secondary or a control, uncorrected.

Eligibility: ≥ 3 usable runs and ≥ 40 trials per condition; all 17 subjects qualified. Channels flagged bad in any subject (CPz, FC1, FC2, PO8) are dropped for everyone, leaving 59 channels.

**Preprocessing changes made after the plan, before any decoding of these subjects** (label-free, documented under "Data facts"): volume-trigger tolerance 3 → 10 samples, grid phase from the most common trigger phase, and cropping on the grid. Without them, sub-07, 10, 11 and 13–17 could not be processed.

### Interpretation

- **The stage-1 hypothesis replicates in new subjects.** Confidence about one's own decision is decodable from EEG in the 0.1–0.6 s after the response, before the rating scale appears, in 16 of 17 subjects, and a model trained on other people transfers to a new person.
- **Not prospective.** Nothing before the decision carries confidence (pre-decision r ≈ 0). The window is post-response, so this is a post-decision signal, not a prediction.
- **Mostly shared with behavior.** The post-decision window also carries the response itself (choice side AUC 0.93) and error-related activity (accuracy AUC 0.58). Removing what accuracy, response latency and evidence explain about confidence halves the within-subject effect (0.16 → 0.08, uncorrected p = 0.013) and removes the cross-subject one. The part that generalises across people is therefore largely the neural correlate of these behavioral variables; a smaller person-specific component remains.
- **Own vs observed.** The effect is clear for own decisions and weak for observed ones (Holm p = 0.18). This fits the paper's own/observed contrast, but the two conditions were not compared directly in the plan, so no claim about the difference is made here.
- **Observed choice side decodes after the "response" (AUC 0.65–0.71)** although participants do not respond in observed trials: the replayed virtual-hand movement is on screen in that window. It is a visual or mirror-motor signal, not the participant's response.
- **Limits:** classical linear decoding of 1–10 Hz amplitudes; no ICA (residual ocular activity is treated as noise); n = 17. The cross-subject result depends on the heartbeat-artifact method (see "Follow-up analyses").

### Heartbeat-artifact method (sensitivity)

`eeg_clean.obs_subtract` implements the optimal basis set (OBS; Niazy et al. 2005: per channel, the mean heartbeat segment plus 4 principal components are fitted to each beat and subtracted). All 19 subjects were re-cleaned with `--bcg-method obs` (QC: [`reports/ds002158-eeg-preprocess-obs.json`](../reports/ds002158-eeg-preprocess-obs.json)) and the confirmatory plan re-run on them ([`reports/ds002158-eeg-decoding-confirmatory-obs.json`](../reports/ds002158-eeg-decoding-confirmatory-obs.json)).

**QC.** OBS leaves less heartbeat-locked activity than template subtraction in every subject (median 0.48 vs 1.02 µV). It also removes more of everything else: channel amplitude in the 1–10 Hz analysis band is 72% of the template-subtraction value (range 47–99%), and the response-locked average drops by 8% (sub-22) to 45% (sub-11). Part of that may be brain activity. With OBS the bad-channel rule flags frontal channels instead (Fp1, Fp2, AF7, AF8, FT10), so 56 channels are used instead of 59.

**Decoding (own decisions, post-decision window).**

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Choice side, within / left-out (AUC) | 0.93 / 0.82 | – | 0.91 / 0.80 |
| Confidence, within subject | r = 0.16, 16/17, Holm 0.0009 | r = 0.16, 16/17, Holm 0.001 | **r = 0.12, 13/17, Holm 0.028** |
| Confidence, left-out subject | r = 0.10, 16/17, Holm 0.005 | r = 0.10, 15/17, Holm 0.009 | **r = 0.04, 12/17, Holm 0.36** |

The channel set is not the cause (middle column, [`configs/ds002158_eeg_sensitivity_obs_channels.yaml`](../configs/ds002158_eeg_sensitivity_obs_channels.yaml)). The method is.

**Cardiac control.** If residual heartbeat artifact carried confidence, the ECG channel itself should. Decoding from the ECG channel alone ([`configs/ds002158_eeg_sensitivity_ecg_only.yaml`](../configs/ds002158_eeg_sensitivity_ecg_only.yaml)): confidence in the post-decision window is not decodable (within r = 0.04, p = 0.16; left-out r = −0.03). Around the response there is a small cross-subject effect (r = 0.05, Holm 0.03). The ECG lead is not purely cardiac: choice side decodes from it after the response (AUC 0.78), i.e. it also picks up movement.

**Reading.**
- The **within-subject** post-decision confidence signal is robust to the artifact method and the channel set.
- The **transfer to new people** is weaker with OBS (4 components). Two explanations were considered: OBS removes trial-related brain activity along with the artifact (its response-locked averages shrink), or template subtraction leaves cardiac activity that varies with confidence (heart rate is known to slow after errors). The analyses below support the first: heartbeat timing does not explain the effect, OBS with 4 components removes about half of a planted response-locked signal, and OBS with 1 component restores the transfer.

### How much brain signal does each method keep? (planted signal, post hoc)

**Reading.**
- The confidence pattern after one's own decisions does **not** carry over to observed decisions, in either direction and with either cleaning method. Within one's own decisions the pattern is reliable, so the absence is not a pipeline failure.
- Response side does not carry over either. After one's own decision it is decoded from the motor act (AUC 0.93); after an observed decision, from a different, weaker signal (0.65), presumably visual. The two conditions share little post-decision EEG structure.
- What this does and does not show:
  - Supported: the post-decision confidence signal measured here is tied to the participant's own action. This could be movement-related activity that covaries with confidence, or a commitment-specific evaluation. These data cannot separate the two.
  - Not shown: that observed decisions lack a confidence signal. Their within-condition signal is weak (r = 0.05), which also limits how well any transfer could be detected.

## Data facts found on download

- **Headers name the original lab files** (`DataFile=s4_run1_09062017.eeg`), not the BIDS names, so MNE cannot open the files as published. `io.brainvision.read_raw` writes a corrected header to a temporary directory and leaves the data untouched.
- 5000 Hz, 64 channels, INT_16; **Ch32 is ECG** but is typed EEG in the header.
- **Synchronised clocks:** `R128` volume triggers are 6400 samples (1.28 s) apart. Seven triggers over the 12 runs deviate by 1–3 samples and return to the grid at the next volume. For sub-04 run-004 (one trigger +2 samples) the artifact was checked directly: subtracting the template at the grid position leaves 45 µV RMS (a normal volume: 43 µV), at the logged position 420 µV. Triggers are therefore snapped to the grid. In the full sample (sub-13 to sub-17) isolated triggers are logged up to 6 samples late. The same check on the worst cases (sub-13 run-002 and sub-15 runs 002/005, deviations of 3–6 samples) found the best-fitting template shift was 0 every time; the residual was 23–33 µV at the grid position vs 470–840 µV at the logged one. The tolerance was therefore raised from 3 to 10 samples (2 ms), before any decoding of the new subjects; larger deviations still stop the pipeline. The grid phase is the most common trigger phase, so a late *first* trigger (sub-07 run-002, 1 sample) does not shift the grid.
- **sub-02 run-004:** the recording stops 0.8 s into the last volume (after the last trial). The incomplete volume is dropped.
- The EEG starts ~2 minutes before the scan. Only the scan period is used.

## Pipeline (`ldm ds002158 eeg-preprocess`)

Per run, without using any trial label:

1. **Pairing** of the EEG run with an events run by marker *timing* (`pair_runs`; every events run of the subject is tried, a pairing must match all trials and be unique).
2. **Gradient artifact:** sliding 21-volume average template subtraction (Allen et al. 2000) at 5000 Hz, locked to the volume grid.
3. **Causal** anti-alias low-pass and decimation to 250 Hz.
4. **BCG artifact:** R peaks on the ECG channel (fallback TP10, the authors' choice, if the ECG rate is implausible), 21-beat average template subtraction (default; `--bcg-method` also offers `obs`, `ica` and `aas_ica`, see "Follow-up analyses"; several methods share one gradient pass).
5. **Causal** 1–10 Hz Butterworth band-pass (the authors' band), bad-channel check (robust z of log SD > 3.5: none found), common average reference.
6. **Epochs** locked to the stimulus marker (−0.2 to 1.2 s) and the response marker (−0.6 to 1.0 s). Response latency comes from the markers, not from `events.tsv`.
7. **Epoch rejection** per subject: log peak-to-peak more than 3.5 robust SDs above the subject median, or above 500 µV.
8. **Per-trial cardiac columns** in `<subject>_trials.csv` (`eeg_clean.cardiac_trial_features`): the heartbeat interval before the stimulus, the interval containing the response and the next two, the post-response change, and the time from the last R peak to the response and from the response to the next R peak. An interval more than 30% from the run median is treated as a detection error and the features using it are left empty.

**Why causal filters.** The authors filtered with a zero-phase (two-pass) filter. That spreads activity backwards in time: a test impulse leaks into the samples before it (`tests/test_eeg.py`). Post-response activity could then appear in a "pre-decision" window. Here every filtered sample depends only on the present and past, so a pre-decision feature cannot contain the response. The price is a frequency-dependent delay, which only makes window labels conservative.

**Not done (differs from the authors):** ICA for ocular artifacts, and dipole-based component selection. ICA for residual BCG was tried afterwards (see "Follow-up analyses"): after template subtraction it finds no heartbeat-locked component.

**Change made before any decoding, documented here:** the initial rejection rule was a fixed 150 µV peak-to-peak threshold. In-scanner epochs have a median peak-to-peak of ~130 µV, so it rejected 31% of sub-04's epochs, mostly because of the mastoid/temporal edge channels (TP9, FT9, FT10). It was replaced by the robust rule above, which uses no labels. With it, 1 of 576 epochs is rejected.

## Decoding (`ldm ds002158 eeg-decode`)

The plan was fixed in [`configs/ds002158_eeg_decoding.yaml`](../configs/ds002158_eeg_decoding.yaml) before any decoding was run.

- **Trials:** valid trials only (author exclusions applied), not rejected, own (active) and observed decisions always analysed separately.
- **Windows** (`datasets.ds002158_eeg.WINDOWS`):
  - **pre-decision:** 0–0.2 s after the stimulus; trials whose response marker comes earlier are dropped (none in valid trials, whose RT ≥ 0.2 s);
  - **response-locked:** −0.2 to +0.1 s around the response;
  - **post-decision:** +0.1 to +0.6 s after the response; ends before the confidence scale (~1.5 s), so it never contains the rating. It does still contain motor execution and its feedback.
- **Features:** mean amplitude per channel in 50 ms bins (63 channels × 4, 6 or 10 bins).
- **Models:** standardisation + L2 logistic regression (AUC) or ridge regression (Spearman r). Regularisation by inner cross-validation within each training fold.
- **Within subject:** leave-one-run-out (6 folds). p-values from 200 permutations of labels *within run*, re-running the whole pipeline (smallest possible p = 0.005).
- **Cross subject:** train on one subject, test on the other; p from 1,000 permutations of the test labels.
- **Primary family:** continuous confidence × 3 windows × 2 conditions, Holm-corrected within each subject.
- **Confidence beyond behavior:** confidence residualised on accuracy, response latency and evidence, with the residualising model fitted on training folds only.

**Reading the scores.** Confidence drifts between runs (run means 0.54–0.69). With leave-one-run-out, a training set's mean then sits on the wrong side of the held-out run's mean, so the pooled held-out correlation is biased negative. The permutation nulls show this (null mean −0.09 to −0.23 for confidence). Compare scores with the null (`null_95`, `p_value`), not with 0. The same effect pushes some AUCs below 0.5.

## Interpretation (stage 1)

- **The pipeline works.** Lateralised motor activity is recovered within and across subjects, only at and after the response, which is what the window definitions predict. Error-related information appears only after own responses.
- **No reliable confidence signal at n = 2.** The pre-specified primary family has no result surviving correction in either subject. The most consistent hint is own decisions, post-decision window (sub-04 within subject; both cross-subject directions). It sits where accuracy is also decodable, and residualising on accuracy, RT and evidence weakens it (within sub-04: r = −0.12, p = 0.80; cross-subject: r = 0.15 / 0.10, p = 0.06 / 0.14). It should be treated as a hypothesis for the full sample, not a finding.
- **Window semantics:** "post-decision" here means after the committed response, not free of it: the window still holds motor execution (choice side decodes at AUC ≥ 0.94). Nothing decoded post-response is a prospective prediction.

## Recommended before scaling to all subjects (stage 1; all done)

1. **Pre-specify a run-mean-free metric** (per-run Spearman averaged over folds, or centring the target within run on training statistics) to remove the run-drift bias. Not applied here, because it was found after the results were seen.
2. Compare the residual BCG with an ICA or OBS approach (done for OBS: see "Follow-up analyses").
3. Download the remaining subjects' raw EEG: ~2 GB per subject, 33 GB for the other 17 subjects (excluding sub-20). **Needs approval.**
